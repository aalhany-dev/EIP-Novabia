THE VALUES INSIDE THE WORLD

Each region carries a concept worth discussing.

The ethical ideas are part of the fiction and gameplay context—not an afterthought added to the pitch.

01 / ELEMENTAL

Care for the environment

Nature is not background scenery. Environmental choices affect creatures, communities and the future they share.

02 / LANDSCAPE

War continues after battle

The consequences of conflict survive ceasefires through damaged places, displaced lives and burdens inherited by people who did not choose the war.

03 / KINETIC

Honor needs the bigger picture

A locally honorable action can unintentionally benefit an enemy or damage the wider community when consequences beyond the immediate group are ignored.

04 / CROSSROADS

Ignored suffering spreads

Neglecting the suffering of one society can deepen resentment, fuel extremism and generate future conflicts that eventually affect others.

05 / FANTASY

Do not generalize individuals

The actions of one companion or person should not become a judgment against an entire group.

06 / GEAR & PROGRESSION

Knowledge is strength

The most capable hero is not automatically the most armored. Books, music, medicine, education, skills and certificates are represented as meaningful forms of power.

READING THE BASKET

Comparable, not representative.

Why these games?

They share creature collection, companion progression or closely related RPG/simulation audiences. This is a curated relevance basket, not the ten best-selling games in the genre.

What can it tell us?

Relative price positioning and an illustrative range of estimated units among selected peers. Using one estimator and platform improves consistency, but does not verify actual publisher revenue.

What can't it tell us?

Novabia's probability of success, expected unit sales or a market-wide median. This selection does not include a measured denominator of lower-selling, unsuccessful or less-visible releases; therefore no genre success-rate claim is made.

PRODUCTION ROADMAP / 04

Measure first.
Focus second. Scale third.

The 18-month roadmap deliberately avoids completing the entire content backlog before market evidence exists. Each stage increases what Novabia knows about players, production and commercial direction.

01
MONTHS 0–3 · STUDIO BUILD

Polish the vertical slice & build the team

Incorporate the company, recruit the distributed team, equip production roles, polish onboarding and game feel, and install analytics/testing infrastructure.

Planned evidence: instrumented end-to-end slice, testing protocol and a repeatable production workflow.

02
MONTHS 3–6 · PLAYER RESEARCH

Measure where attachment becomes strongest

External testing measures technical friction, battle balance, progression clarity, companion attachment, evolution curiosity and repeat-play behavior.

Planned evidence: tester-observation report with first baselines, friction points and a prioritized improvement backlog.

03
MONTHS 6–9 · COMMERCIAL VALIDATION

Measure positioning & economics

Launch the Steam presence, test creative messages and audiences, acquire wishlists, measure CAC signals, compare regional response and test willingness to pay.

Planned evidence: measured Steam visits, attributed wishlist additions, cost-per-wishlist experiments and audience/creative comparisons.

04
MONTHS 9–12 · SCALE STRONGEST SIGNALS

Expand what evidence says matters most

Scale roads, companions, story, systems and production tools according to the player and market signals showing the greatest potential.

Planned evidence: actual content-production time/cost per milestone and evidence-based choices about what to expand.

05
MONTH 12 · NEXT ROUND PREPARATION

Raise before runway becomes pressure

Begin the next financing process approximately six months before the first runway ends, now using actual playtest, wishlist, acquisition and production evidence.

Planned evidence: updated investor data room, runway model and financing options informed by measured results.

06
MONTHS 12–18 · LAUNCH READINESS

Production, QA, localization & commercial preparation

Continue validated content expansion, optimize performance, deepen QA, grow community, prepare launch assets and reach a release-candidate state or the next funded production stage.

Planned evidence: QA/performance results and an evidence-based launch-readiness or further-production decision.

MEASUREMENT DESIGN

First establish the baseline. Then decide what good looks like.

Funding supports a structured learning program rather than pre-selecting impressive-looking percentages. The numbers collected at each gate shape the next phase's objectives and allocation.

01 / OBSERVE

Explore real player behavior

Recruit a small discovery cohort, observe the complete play journey and record where curiosity, attachment or friction occurs. A first group of roughly 10–15 is a planning example, not a reported test or statistical sample.

02 / COMPARE

Test improvements and segments

Use follow-up cohorts—potentially 30–50 as an initial recruitment planning range—to compare onboarding, gameplay moments and audience messages. Set meaningful benchmarks only after observing variation and establishing baseline measurements.

03 / ALLOCATE

Invest where signals improve

Track session completion, repeat play, attachment, wishlist acquisition, production velocity and cost. Redirect content and marketing spending toward the strongest evidence, while explicitly recording remaining uncertainty.

These cohort sizes are proposed research planning ranges, not established traction, guaranteed recruitment quantities or statistically representative samples. Steam visits and wishlist costs are prelaunch signals; paying-customer acquisition cost and sales conversion can only be assessed after commercial purchases occur.
